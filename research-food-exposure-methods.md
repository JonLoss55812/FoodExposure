# Child Food Exposure Methods Research Summary

## 1. "So Your Tongue Can Learn" Phrase

The exact phrase "So Your Tongue Can Learn" did not return direct search results as a named method or program. However, the concept aligns closely with **taste education** and **sensory food exposure** approaches used in pediatric feeding therapy. The phrase likely encapsulates the core idea behind repeated food exposure: that taste preferences are *learned* through repeated, low-pressure sensory experiences. The tongue literally "learns" to accept new foods through systematic, graduated exposure.

## 2. Key Established Methods

### A. SOS Approach to Feeding (Sequential Oral Sensory)
- **Creator:** Dr. Kay Toomey
- **Type:** Transdisciplinary feeding therapy program
- **Core Idea:** Children learn to eat through a systematic, sensory-based hierarchy
- **Assessment Areas:** Organs, muscles, sensory, learning, development, nutrition, environment
- **Foundation:** Systematic desensitization + normal developmental blueprint

### B. Food Chaining
- **Creators:** Cheri Fraker, Dr. Mark Fishbein, Sibyl Cox, Laura Walbert
- **Book:** *Food Chaining: The Proven 6-Step Plan to Stop Picky Eating*
- **Core Idea:** Start with accepted foods, then introduce similar foods varying by one property (taste, texture, temperature, color, shape)
- **Key Rule:** No hiding food, no pressure; child always knows what they are eating

### C. The Three E's (Melanie Potock)
- **Creator:** Melanie Potock, MA, CCC-SLP
- **Steps:** Expose, Explore, Expand
- **Tracking:** One tablespoon per exposure, at least 3x per week, track up to 15 exposures per food
- **Tool:** Chart-based system where foods move from "approach" column to "accepted" column

### D. 5 Levels of Food Exposure (Kids Feeding Wellness)
- **Target:** Children ages 2+
- **Core Idea:** Build "food trust" — the internal sense that food is safe, predictable, and worth exploring
- **Approach:** Step-by-step protocol for cautious eaters to progress at their own pace

## 3. Core Principles (Common Across Methods)

1. **No pressure** — Never force, coerce, bribe, or guilt a child into eating
2. **Repeated neutral exposure** — 8-15+ exposures needed for acceptance (research-backed)
3. **Multisensory progression** — Engage all senses: sight, sound, smell, touch, taste
4. **Small steps** — Progress through a hierarchy from tolerance to eating
5. **Child-led pace** — The child sets the speed of progression
6. **Playful interaction** — Food exploration should be fun, not stressful
7. **Preferred foods always available** — Safe/accepted foods served alongside new foods
8. **Quality over quantity** — Better to have positive interactions than large volumes eaten
9. **Consistency** — Regular, repeated opportunities matter more than single big attempts

---

## 4. Number of Exposures Typically Needed

| Population | Exposures Needed | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Typical children | **8-15 exposures** | Research-backed across multiple studies |
| Picky eaters | **15-20+ exposures** | May need significantly more |
| Problem feeders / ARFID | **20-30+ exposures** | Often require professional therapy |

**Critical finding:** Most parents give up after only 3-5 attempts. The gap between typical parental persistence (3-5) and what research shows is needed (8-15+) is a key problem.

An "exposure" is NOT just tasting — it includes seeing, smelling, touching, and being in proximity to the food.

## 5. Stages/Steps of Food Exposure

### SOS Approach: 32 Steps to Eating (6 Categories)

1. **Tolerate** — Food in the room, on the table, on the plate
2. **Interact** — Touch food with utensils, move it around
3. **Smell** — Lean in, sniff, describe the smell
4. **Touch** — With hands, on skin, on lips
5. **Taste** — Lick, place on tongue, hold in mouth
6. **Eat** — Bite, chew, swallow

### Detailed 12-Step Graduated Hierarchy (from research)

1. Tolerate food in same room
2. Tolerate food on shared table
3. Tolerate food placed directly in front of child
4. Touch food, pick up, throw in garbage
5. Pick up food, smell it, throw away
6. Pick up food, kiss food, dispose
7. Lick food one time (tongue contact)
8. Lick food five consecutive times
9. Break food using teeth into two pieces
10. Chew food five times, then dispose
11. Eat one small piece (eraser-sized)
12. Eat one full piece (tablespoon-sized)

### Food Chaining: 6-Step Plan

1. Identify all currently accepted foods
2. Analyze properties of accepted foods (taste, texture, temperature, color, shape)
3. Select target foods similar to accepted foods
4. Make one small change at a time (e.g., different brand of same food)
5. Gradually increase the difference between accepted and target foods
6. Rate acceptance 1-5; if low, make smaller changes; if high, continue

### Playful Eats App: 4-Step System

1. **Exchange** — Swap similar foods
2. **Expose** — Present new foods without pressure
3. **Explore** — Interact with food through senses
4. **Expand** — Grow the food repertoire

## 6. Data an App Should Track

### Per Food Item
- Food name and category (protein, vegetable, fruit, grain, dairy)
- Preparation method (raw, cooked, fried, etc.)
- Temperature served (hot, warm, room temp, cold)
- Texture (smooth, crunchy, soft, chewy, mixed)
- Current stage in hierarchy (tolerate / interact / smell / touch / taste / eat)
- Number of exposures to date (counter toward 15+)
- Date and time of each exposure
- Acceptance rating per exposure (1-5 scale)
- Child's reaction notes (e.g., "touched but refused to smell," "licked once")
- Whether preferred/safe foods were also offered

### Per Meal/Session
- Date and time
- Meal type (breakfast, lunch, dinner, snack)
- Setting (home, school, restaurant, therapy)
- Foods presented (both safe and new)
- Duration of meal
- Child's mood/state before meal
- Pressure level (none, mild encouragement, etc.)
- Who was present

### Progress Tracking (Over Time)
- Total number of accepted foods (food repertoire size)
- Foods in each stage of the hierarchy
- Exposure count per food (progress toward 8-15 threshold)
- Weekly/monthly exposure frequency
- Movement between stages (e.g., food moved from "touch" to "taste")
- Food chains created (which accepted foods linked to which new foods)
- Regression tracking (foods that moved backward)
- Streaks and consistency metrics

### Child Profile
- Age
- Known allergies or medical conditions
- Sensory sensitivities (texture, temperature, color preferences)
- Safe/preferred foods list
- Rejected foods list with reasons
- Therapy involvement (SOS, OT, SLP, etc.)
- Goals (e.g., "try 3 new vegetables this month")

### Reporting / Insights
- Visual progress charts showing hierarchy movement
- Exposure frequency heatmaps
- Success rate by food category, texture, temperature
- Pattern detection (e.g., "child accepts crunchy foods more readily")
- Shareable reports for therapists/pediatricians
- Milestone celebrations (e.g., "10th new food accepted!")
